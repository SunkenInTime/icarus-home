Three faces, one mark, all caps

Round one settled the case: caps. It also settled the shortlist. This page works only those three faces, pushes each through its weights and widths, puts the mark in for the A, tries stealing the letters you liked from one face into another, and then shrinks everything down to where a wordmark actually has to live.

Pick 1 · Geist 600Icarus
You liked the R. Straight leg, no flick. Sibling of the Geist Mono labels already on the site.
Pick 2 · Saira Expanded 600Icarus
You liked the C and the R. Squared C with flat terminals, R with a kicked leg. Widest of the three.
Pick 3 · Outfit 700Icarus
You liked it small. Round C, pointed A, dense at 18px. Most "product", least "badge".
01 · Sweeps

Each face pushed through its range

Same face, different weight or width. Weight changes how much the word asserts itself; width changes how much it feels like flight. Saira has a real width axis so it gets both. Tracking is tuned per row so the rhythm stays even; nudge it from the top bar.

Saira

Read on it: Geist wants to stay at 500 or 600; at 700 the R closes up. Saira gets better as it gets wider, and 112 is a useful middle if 125 feels like too much wingspan. Outfit 700 is its sweet spot; 800 gets blobby, 600 loses the density you liked small.
02 · The mark is the A

IC▲RUS

The mark already has an apex and a wide base, so it wants to be an A more than an I. Three sizes per face: cap sits inside the cap height like a letter, overshoot pokes above the cap line the way a real A's apex does, and violet is the one place the command colour is allowed, as a single object rather than a wash.

Read on it: the mark reads as an A most convincingly with the overshoot, because the eye expects an A to be the tallest letter. With Saira Expanded the mark looks narrow next to the wide letters, so it needs a small horizontal bump in that lockup. In Outfit, whose A is already pointed, the swap is nearly seamless.
03 · Hybrids

Stealing letters

You liked Saira's C and R and Geist's R. A wordmark ships as drawn vectors anyway, not as a font, so mixing letters is legitimate: the end product is an SVG. Cap heights are matched at runtime by measuring each face, then the borrowed letters are scaled to fit. Rough, but enough to judge whether the mix wants to exist.

Read on it: the honest question is whether Saira's C and R make Geist better or just make it look like Saira. If the mix wins, the next step is drawing the six letters properly rather than shipping two font files.
04 · Size ladder

Down to where it lives

Site nav is 18px. The app title bar is 12px. A Windows taskbar tooltip is 11px. Left column is the plain word, right columns swap the mark in for the A. Tracking widens slightly as size drops, which is what keeps caps readable small.

Read on it: this is the test that decides it. If the mark-as-A holds at 12px, it can be the wordmark everywhere. If it doesn't, the rule becomes: plain caps small, mark-as-A only at splash and hero size.
