What should the word ICARUS look like?

Everything below is the same six letters set different ways. Click any card to push it into the context mocks at the bottom (nav, title bar, splash, hero). Pin cards to line them up in a compare tray. The top bar switches case and nudges tracking across every card at once, so the caps question can be answered by looking rather than arguing.

Ground rules I worked from: the mark stays. Violet stays the one command colour, so it appears in the wordmark only as a deliberate accent, never as the whole word. Nothing here should read as a generic gamer overlay, and the wordmark has to survive at 18px in a nav and at 11px in a title bar.

The wordmark is a label that grew up

02 · Wingspan

Wide letters, arms out

Extended caps read as flight, aerospace, and hardware badges. It is the most physical way to say Icarus without drawing a wing. Tracking stays tight because the letters are already wide; the width does the work.

03 · Ascent

Tall, condensed, vertical

Condensed caps are the native tongue of esports and of Valorant itself. It says "competitive" instantly. The danger is that it says it in the same voice as every tournament overlay, so the version that works has to be restrained: one weight, no slant, no stencil cuts.

Sporty and sharp with distinctive angled terminals. Feels like a team crest. Strongest personality in this row. IcarusIcarus
Oswald 600tracking +0.08em
Icarus
The classic gothic condensed. Reliable and legible, but it is everywhere. IcarusIcarus
Bebas Neuetracking +0.10em

Read on it: Barlow Condensed is the only one I'd defend. Condensed caps put Icarus next to Valorant's own type, which is either the point or the problem. It helps users recognise the world, but it also makes Icarus look like a fan project rather than a tool.
04 · Everyday

Soft geometric, the tool you open daily

The site's own words: "honestly just nice to use every day". Onest and Marlin Soft are already this voice. Caps in a soft geometric are friendlier than a grotesk and less costume than a serif. This is the continuity option: change the case and tracking, keep the family.

05 · Myth

Serif caps, the Greek in the name

Icarus is a story before it is a product. A serif wordmark over a brutally functional dark UI is a real contrast, and contrast is what makes a brand memorable. The site's copy already leans this way: "closer to the sun", "touch the sun". The trap is the temple-and-toga look, so the serif has to be sharp and modern, not carved in marble.

06 · Terminal

Mono caps, icarus.exe

Take the callsign idea to its end: the wordmark is literally set in the label face. Local-first, open source, MIT, a file on your machine. The mono says all of that. The question is whether the name of the product should sound like its metadata.

07 · Letterform ideas

Small moves inside the word

These are treatments, not fonts. Each one is shown on Archivo Expanded as a neutral base, but most would work on any of the directions above. The good ones give the wordmark a reason to exist beyond typing the name. The rule: one move, never two.

The sun in the C
ICARUS
A single violet dot sitting in the aperture of the C. The sun he flew toward. Also the one place violet is allowed in the wordmark: it's a point, not a wash.
The A with no crossbar
ICARUS
Remove the bar and the A becomes a peak, a wing, or a fall depending on your mood. Cheap, distinctive, and it survives at every size.
The mark is the I
CARUS
The flame-wing mark stands in for the I. Ties mark and wordmark into one object. Needs the mark to be a bit narrower than it is today to sit right.
ICARVS
ICARVS
Roman V for U. Instantly classical without a serif. Some people will read it as a typo. That's either the charm or the problem.
Flight path underline
ICARUS
The dashed movement line users draw on maps, under the name. The brand borrows from the product's own pen. Works as an animated reveal on the site.
Stacked lockup
ICARUSStrats
Name up top, "STRATS" as a mono callsign beneath. Solves the "Icarus Strats" vs "Icarus" naming question: one is the wordmark, the other is the label.
Version tag
ICARUSv4.6.1
The version rides alongside in mono. Only for the title bar and about screen, but it shows how the wordmark and the callsign voice coexist.
Horizon
ICARUS
A hairline leading in, an ember dot after. Wordmark as a line of flight with the sun at the end. Quiet, and it uses the map ember instead of violet.
Interpunct
I·C·A·R·U·S
Letters separated by dots, like the site's own "FREE · MIT · V4.6.1". Very tactical. Probably too much for the primary mark, good for a footer.
The fall
ICARUS
Each letter a step lower than the last. It's the story in one gesture. It's also a joke the brand can only tell once, and it breaks alignment in a nav.
Outline with one solid
ICARUS
Hollow letters with one filled. Wireframe is the language of the app's map view. Risky at small sizes; hero and splash only.
Boxed
ICARUS
A thin keyline around the word. Badge, plate, stencil. Reads clearly as a logo rather than as a heading, which matters on a page full of headings.
Read on it: the sun-in-the-C, the crossbar-less A, and the flight-path underline are the three I'd actually push further. Each is one move, each has a reason from the story or the product, and each survives being shrunk.
